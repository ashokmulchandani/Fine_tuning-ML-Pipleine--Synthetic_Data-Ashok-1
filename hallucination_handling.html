How to Handle Hallucinations

LLMs invent facts — detection finds them, prevention stops them. Here is every technique, where it lives in our code, and the priority order to implement them.

1. Detection vs Prevention — They Are Not the Same

Our current code detects hallucinations after training. It does NOT prevent the model from hallucinating.

ApproachWhat It DoesWhen It WorksLimitation
Detection (assertion tests)Catches KNOWN fake terms: Metformix, DiabetixAfter training, before deploymentOnly catches what you predict. Unknown hallucinations pass through.
Prevention (DPO, RAG, guardrails)Stops the model from inventing facts AT ALLDuring training (DPO) or during inference (RAG)DPO = one-time training cost. RAG = ongoing infra cost.
Testing alone is not enough. The anti-hallucination test catches "Metformix" — but what about unknown fake drug names you CANNOT predict? Prevention (DPO) teaches the model the PATTERN of honesty, not just a blacklist of fake words.

4. Context Length & Hallucination

Training (params.yaml)Serving (vLLM)Problem
Context Windowmax_seq_length: 512--max-model-len 2048Serving context is 4x larger than training context
What Model Sees30-80 tokens per Q&A
"### Instruction: Q ### Response: A"		Up to 2000 tokens
Long conversation + complex question		Model is in "uncharted territory" beyond 512 tokens
Hallucination RiskLow (in-distribution)Higher (out-of-distribution beyond 512)Model never trained on long contexts → invents to fill the gap
FixServe at training context: vllm serve --max-model-len 512But limits long conversations
The trade-off: Shorter context = less hallucination. Longer context = more flexible conversations. For medical/legal Q&A (where accuracy matters more than creativity), serve at 512. For chatbots, accept the risk at 2048.
